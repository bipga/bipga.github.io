Introduction to partial graph alignment

Graph alignment identifies corresponding vertices across two input graphs. In bilateral partial graph alignment, each graph can contain private vertices with no counterpart in the other graph. A useful output therefore includes both a correspondence decision and a decision about which vertices should participate in the alignment.

Inputs and predictions

Dataset.load reads graph topology and a reference mapping. It preserves explicitly listed isolated vertices and reindexes integer vertex IDs to local, contiguous IDs. The returned AlignmentDataset retains the mapping back to the original identifiers.

An Algorithm receives the two topology graphs through align(graph1, graph2). Its AlignmentResult contains native predicted pairs and, when available, continuous scores, costs or embeddings.

Construction and inference

bpupga.construct() creates a controlled partial pair by partitioning original matched identities into shared, source-only and target-only groups. The requested source and target overlap ratios are rounded to integer group sizes and the realized values are recorded in metadata.

References remain outside unsupervised inference. Native predictions are evaluated after the method returns; diagnostic analyses that use reference information are explicitly post-inference analyses.

Evaluate more than ranking

Pair precision, recall and F1 describe correspondence recovery. Node matchability scores describe selection of participating vertices. Validity checks reveal repeated endpoints and other feasibility issues. Hits@K and MRR describe rankings over reference-matched queries when the output supplies an appropriate ranking representation.

A high ranking score alone does not measure rejection of private vertices. Incomplete references also cannot establish that every unannotated vertex is truly unmatched; the evaluator reports the available reference scope.
