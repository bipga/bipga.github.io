Introduction to partial graph alignment
=======================================

Graph alignment identifies corresponding vertices across two input graphs.
In **bilateral partial graph alignment**, each graph can contain private
vertices with no counterpart in the other graph. A useful output therefore
includes both a correspondence decision and a decision about which vertices
should participate in the alignment.

.. figure:: _static/alignment.svg
   :alt: Two graphs with three corresponding vertex pairs and private vertices on both sides.
   :width: 650px

   Shared vertices have a counterpart; private vertices occur on only one side.

Inputs and predictions
----------------------

``Dataset.load`` reads graph topology and a reference mapping. It preserves
explicitly listed isolated vertices and reindexes integer vertex IDs to local,
contiguous IDs. The returned :class:`~bpupga.AlignmentDataset` retains the
mapping back to the original identifiers.

An :class:`~bpupga.Algorithm` receives the two topology graphs through
``align(graph1, graph2)``. Its :class:`~bpupga.AlignmentResult` contains native
predicted pairs and, when available, continuous scores, costs or embeddings.

Construction and inference
--------------------------

:func:`bpupga.construct` creates a controlled partial pair by partitioning
original matched identities into shared, source-only and target-only groups.
The requested source and target overlap ratios are rounded to integer group
sizes and the realized values are recorded in metadata.

References remain outside unsupervised inference. Native predictions are
evaluated after the method returns; diagnostic analyses that use reference
information are explicitly post-inference analyses.

Evaluate more than ranking
--------------------------

Pair precision, recall and F1 describe correspondence recovery. Node
matchability scores describe selection of participating vertices. Validity
checks reveal repeated endpoints and other feasibility issues. Hits@K and MRR
describe rankings over reference-matched queries when the output supplies an
appropriate ranking representation.

A high ranking score alone does not measure rejection of private vertices.
Incomplete references also cannot establish that every unannotated vertex is
truly unmatched; the evaluator reports the available reference scope.
